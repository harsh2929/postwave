# Voice AI

Voice AI solves the **activation problem**: ~80% of newsletter operators on every ESP we've studied never set up a single automated sequence. The reason is rarely "they don't want to" — it's that writing 5–10 emails up front is too much work and feels too high-stakes to do without a clear voice match.

Voice AI fixes both halves: it generates the drafts, and it does it in *your* voice — not a generic SaaS-marketing voice — by training on a fingerprint of how you actually write.

## The voice-fingerprint approach

A "voice profile" in Moosewave is **not** a fine-tuned model. It's a structured summary of *how you write* extracted from your past content:

- Stylistic features (sentence length distribution, paragraph cadence, openings, sign-offs).
- Vocabulary (your recurring words, your avoided words, your specific phrases).
- Structural patterns (do you use lists? subheaders? bold? em-dashes?).
- Tonal range (formal/informal, warm/direct, humour density).
- Persona attributes (1st-person vs editorial-we, expertise framing).

Generation conditions a base LLM on this profile plus a chosen [template](/voice-ai/templates), then scores each output for [fidelity](/voice-ai/fidelity-scoring) and auto-retries below threshold.

## What this is good for

- **First sequences** — welcome series, onboarding, re-engagement. Hardest to start; easiest to ship with AI scaffolding.
- **Template-shaped content** — launch sequences, course sales, year-in-review. Patterns are well-known; voice match is the differentiator.
- **Drafts to edit** — Voice AI is best as a 70% solution; you do the last mile.

## What it's not good for

See [Best practices](/voice-ai/best-practices) for the do/don't list. The short version: **don't ship crisis communications, regulatory disclosures, or anything personal-relationship-sensitive without editing**.
