# Voice AI — best practices

Voice AI is a force multiplier when used well and an embarrassment when used poorly. The principles below are extracted from watching ~400 newsletter operators ship AI-drafted sequences over the last year.

## When AI can write the first draft

Strong fits:

- **Welcome sequences** — pattern is well-known, voice match matters most, low downside if a draft is mid.
- **Re-engagement** — formulaic by nature; the whole point is the recipient hasn't engaged in a while, so polished-but-generic is fine.
- **Course / product launch sequences** — structure is well-established; AI fills it in faster than you can.
- **SaaS onboarding** — high-volume, low-personal sequences that benefit from systematic coverage.
- **Year-in-review, monthly-changelog, status-update** — formulaic; voice match is the entire game.
- **Cross-promo, sponsorship pitches** — short, structured, low-stakes.

In all of these: **AI drafts → you edit → ship.** Expect to spend 5–15 minutes editing per email. Less than that and you probably haven't read closely enough.

## When AI shouldn't write unedited

Hard cases:

- **Crisis communications.** Apology emails, security incident disclosures, layoffs, pivots. AI-generated apologies systematically miss the *specificity* that makes an apology feel real. Use the [crisis templates](/voice-ai/templates) as scaffolds, but rewrite at least 50% of the words.
- **Personal-relationship emails.** Birthday emails to a long-time subscriber, a personal note about a milestone, a 1:1 follow-up. AI can't fake the relationship.
- **Regulatory / legal disclosures.** Privacy policy updates, GDPR notices, terms changes. Use a lawyer-reviewed template, not AI.
- **Anything that pretends to be 1:1.** "Hey, I noticed you opened my last email…" written by AI is unethical and recipients increasingly recognise it. If it's automated, frame it as automated.

## The "70% rule"

Treat AI output as a 70% solution. The remaining 30% — the parts that make it *yours* — is the editing pass. Specifically:

- **Replace the opener.** AI openers tend toward "Hey `{name}`!" or "Quick update —". Your opener should reference something specific to *this moment, this audience*.
- **Replace one paragraph with a personal anecdote.** Even just 2 sentences. It's the highest-leverage edit you can make.
- **Cut the AI's CTA framing.** AI defaults to "Click here to learn more" / "I'd love your thoughts!". Your CTAs should be specific: "Reply with the one thing you wish I'd cover next" beats "Hit reply with feedback" by 4× engagement.

## Don'ts

- **Don't ship a sequence without reading every email.** Generation can subtly hallucinate facts about your product, your launch dates, your pricing.
- **Don't chase fidelity scores.** A 65 you've edited beats a 90 you haven't.
- **Don't generate from a thin profile.** If your [profile](/voice-ai/profile) was built from &lt;5 sources, the output will be generic. Ingest more first.
- **Don't use the same template every quarter.** Recipients notice repetition. Rotate.

<Callout type="warning">
**The compounding mistake.** Operators who ship AI-drafted sequences without editing for 3+ months see a measurable engagement decline of 15–40%. The signal recipients pick up on isn't "this is AI" — it's *sameness*. AI outputs vary less than human writing; your audience's brain pattern-matches to it.
</Callout>

## A workflow that works

1. **Plan the sequence on paper** (what's the goal of each email?).
2. **Generate** with the matching template.
3. **Read all emails together** before editing any single one — catches structural issues.
4. **Edit each email**: replace opener, swap a paragraph for an anecdote, sharpen the CTA.
5. **Re-read in order.** Does the sequence build? Does each email pay off the previous one?
6. **Schedule the first send 24h out**, not immediately. Sleep on it.
7. **Send.**
