# Spam protection

Public forms are spam magnets. Moosewave layers three protections; you choose which to enable per form.

## Honeypot (always on)

A hidden form field that humans never see (CSS `display: none`, plus `aria-hidden`, plus an off-screen position) but naive scraper-bots gleefully fill in. Submissions with the honeypot field populated are silently rejected.

**Catches**: ~70–85% of automated form spam. Good enough that most low-volume forms don't need anything else.

## Optional reCAPTCHA v3

For forms with high spam volume, enable Google reCAPTCHA v3 (invisible — no "click the traffic lights" challenge unless score is borderline).

To enable:

1. Get a v3 site-key + secret-key from [google.com/recaptcha/admin](https://www.google.com/recaptcha/admin).
2. **Forms → \<form\> → Settings → Spam protection** → toggle reCAPTCHA, paste keys.
3. Choose the score threshold (default `0.5`; `0.3` more permissive, `0.7` stricter).

reCAPTCHA scores 0.0–1.0; submissions below the threshold are rejected.

<Callout type="info">
reCAPTCHA introduces a Google network call on every form load, which has Core Web Vitals impact. Honeypot + IP rate-limit handles 95% of cases without it. Don't enable reCAPTCHA preemptively.
</Callout>

## IP rate limit

Hard-coded protection: **10 submissions per IP per minute**, per form. Excess submissions return `429 Too Many Requests`.

This is non-configurable (we don't want to give operators a footgun). 10/min is high enough that no legitimate human ever hits it, low enough that scraper-driven spam loops get stopped fast.

## Email-domain blocklist

Moosewave maintains a managed blocklist of known disposable / spam-trap email domains (mailinator.com, guerrillamail.com, etc.). Submissions to those domains are rejected with a friendly "please use a permanent email" message.

You can add custom rules under **Settings → Workspace → Subscriber rules**:

- Block specific domains.
- Block specific addresses.
- Require email to match a regex (rare; useful for B2B forms enforcing corp emails).

## Best practices

- **Always honeypot.** It's free.
- **Always double opt-in** for public forms (see [Opt-in](/forms/opt-in)). Even if a bot signs up a real person's email, the confirmation step blocks them from being added.
- **Add reCAPTCHA only if** honeypot + double opt-in is leaking enough spam to matter.
- **Monitor your `pending` count.** If pending sign-ups suddenly spike with low confirm-rates, you're being attacked. Toggle reCAPTCHA on and the attack typically stops within hours.
