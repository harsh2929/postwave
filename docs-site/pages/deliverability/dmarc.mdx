# DMARC — the policy layer

DMARC builds on SPF and DKIM. It tells receivers two things: (1) what to do when SPF and DKIM both fail (`p=` policy), and (2) where to send aggregate forensic reports about who's sending using your domain (`rua=`).

Without DMARC, SPF and DKIM are advisory. With DMARC, they become enforceable.

## Policy strength

The `p=` tag has three values:

| Policy        | What receivers do on failure                                            | When to use                                                           |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `p=none`      | Nothing — but they still send aggregate reports.                        | **Monitoring mode.** Always start here. Use for 1–2 weeks minimum.    |
| `p=quarantine` | Mark the message as suspicious — typically routed to spam folder.       | After monitoring shows no legitimate `fail`s, promote to this.        |
| `p=reject`    | Bounce the message at SMTP time. The sender sees a hard delivery failure. | The endgame for protected brands. Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules at &gt;5k/day. |

## Alignment

DMARC requires the domain in the visible `From:` header to **align** with either:

- The domain that signed via DKIM (`d=` in the DKIM-Signature header), or
- The `MAIL FROM` (envelope-sender / Return-Path) domain that SPF validated.

Alignment can be `relaxed` (subdomain match, default) or `strict` (exact match).

> Example of relaxed alignment passing:
> - `From: alice@news.yourdomain.com`
> - DKIM `d=yourdomain.com`
> - → relaxed alignment passes (organizational domain matches).

## Aggregate reports (RUA)

Set `rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com` (or any address). Receiving providers will send you a daily XML report listing every IP that sent mail claiming to be your domain, plus per-IP SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail counts.

Moosewave's DMARC dashboard (under **Deliverability → DMARC**) **parses these reports automatically** if you point `rua=` at `dmarc@<your-workspace>.dmarc.moosewave.com`. You'll see:

- Daily volume by source IP.
- SPF/DKIM pass rates per source.
- Forwarders, third-party senders (e.g., calendar invites, helpdesk), and *spoofers* — clearly separated.
- "Ready to promote to p=quarantine" indicator when 14+ consecutive days show 100% pass on legitimate sources.

## Promoting from p=none to p=quarantine — safely

The point of `p=none` is to discover senders you didn't know about (your CRM, your support desk, calendar invites, your CFO's vacation auto-responder). Promoting too early breaks legitimate mail.

A safe promotion path:

1. **Week 0** — publish `p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@<workspace>.dmarc.moosewave.com`.
2. **Weeks 1–2** — Moosewave parses inbound aggregate reports. Review the **Senders** tab. For every legitimate source that's failing alignment, fix it (add an SPF include, or get DKIM signing enabled at that source).
3. **Week 3** — when the dashboard shows 14 days of green across all known legitimate senders, promote to `p=quarantine; pct=25`. The `pct=25` means only 25% of failing mail is quarantined — a partial rollout that limits blast radius.
4. **Week 4** — bump to `pct=100`.
5. **Week 6+** — once `quarantine` is uneventful, promote to `p=reject`.

<Callout type="warning">
**Don't skip the monitoring window.** Going straight from no DMARC to `p=reject` will quietly break mail you didn't realise was being sent on your behalf. Recovery is a week of customer-support pain.
</Callout>

## Subdomain policy (`sp=`)

By default, subdomains inherit the parent's policy. If you want a stricter policy on subdomains (e.g., your apex sends marketing but your subdomains should never send), explicitly set `sp=reject`.

## What Moosewave's validator checks

1. Record exists at `_dmarc.<your-domain>`.
2. Exactly one record.
3. Policy strength (yellow for `none`, green for `quarantine`/`reject`).
4. RUA address present and points somewhere monitored (preferably us, so the dashboard works).
5. Alignment of recent Moosewave sends — sampled from the receiving side.
