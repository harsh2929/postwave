# Blacklists (DNSBLs)

DNS Block Lists are public — usually free — databases of IP addresses and domains that have been observed sending spam. Receiving mailservers query them in real time. Being listed on a major DNSBL means a meaningful chunk of receivers will silently reject or junk your mail.

## The DNSBLs Moosewave monitors

| List           | What it catches                                                       | Severity |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| **Spamhaus ZEN** (SBL/CSS/XBL/PBL/DBL) | The biggest. Most receivers consult it.                | 🔴 Critical |
| **SORBS**      | Aggregator of multiple sub-lists; older but widely consulted.        | 🟠 High  |
| **Barracuda**  | Operator-specific list; affects mail to Barracuda-protected receivers (a lot of corporate). | 🟠 High |
| **Invaluement** | Subscription list popular with enterprise spam filters.             | 🟡 Medium |
| **SpamCop**    | Complaint-driven; listings are usually short-lived (24h).            | 🟡 Medium |
| **URIBL** / **SURBL** | Domain-name lists (not IP). Listing your *sending* or *clickable-link* domain is severe. | 🔴 Critical |
| **UCEPROTECT L1/L2/L3** | Aggressive listing; widely considered noisy. We monitor but don't surface as critical. | 🟢 Info |

## Continuous monitoring

Moosewave queries each list **every 6 hours** for:

- Your sending IP(s).
- Your sending domain.
- Your tracking subdomain.
- Any link-host domain in your last 100 sends.

A new listing triggers:

1. An immediate alert (email + dashboard).
2. A [Diagnostician](/deliverability/diagnostician) entry with the listing details and a delisting fix.
3. An automatic pause of large outbound campaigns until you acknowledge.

## Delisting workflow

When listed, the Diagnostician's fix walks you through delisting. Generic steps:

### 1. Find the cause first

**Don't request delisting before you know why you were listed.** Most lists keep an internal record of "delisted recently" and re-listing without remediation triggers a longer cool-off.

The Diagnostician will surface the most likely cause from your recent activity (volume spike, complaint cluster, suspect URL in a recent send, etc.).

### 2. Remediate

Common remediations:

- Pause campaigns to high-complaint segments.
- Drop hard-bounced addresses across all segments.
- Remove a flagged URL from your link library and from any ongoing automation.
- For URIBL/SURBL listings on a *click-tracking* host: rotate the tracking domain (Moosewave provisions a new subdomain in &lt;5 min).

### 3. Submit delisting request

Each list has a delisting form:

- **Spamhaus**: [https://www.spamhaus.org/lookup/](https://www.spamhaus.org/lookup/) — search your IP/domain, follow the "remove from list" link. They typically delist within 24h if remediation is plausible.
- **SORBS**: requires an account; manual review can take 48–72h.
- **Barracuda**: [https://www.barracudacentral.org/rbl/removal-request](https://www.barracudacentral.org/rbl/removal-request) — usually fast (1–4h) if the cause is no longer evident.
- **SpamCop**: auto-delists 24h after the last reported complaint; rarely worth manually requesting.
- **URIBL/SURBL**: domain-level; submit at the list's removal URL. May require proof you control the domain.

Moosewave's Diagnostician fix has a *Pre-fill delisting form* button that copies your IP/domain and a remediation summary you can paste into the form.

<Callout type="info">
For the **PBL** sub-list of Spamhaus ZEN, listings are actually network-policy declarations from the IP's owner ("this IP is not authorised to send mail directly"). Moosewave's IPs are pre-cleared on the PBL. If you ever see a PBL listing on your account, contact support — it's our problem, not yours.
</Callout>

### 4. Confirm delisting

After requesting, Moosewave's monitor will detect delisting within the next 6-hour cycle. You can also force a manual recheck from the dashboard.

## Avoiding listings in the first place

The single best defense is consistent application of the [Improving placement](/deliverability/improving-placement) playbook — list hygiene, suppression management, complaint-rate discipline. Listings almost never come from clean senders.
